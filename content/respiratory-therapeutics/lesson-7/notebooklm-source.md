## Topic 2: Intermittent Positive Pressure Breathing

- Intermittent Positive Pressure Breathing is abbreviated IPPB.
- IPPB delivers short-duration lung expansion therapy.
- The source also calls this hyperinflation therapy.

**IPPB indications:**

- Evidence of atelectasis on chest X-ray.
- Ineffective cough that is non-productive.
- Neuromuscular disorders.
- Kyphoscoliosis.
- Muscle fatigue or weakness.
- Respiratory muscle exercise.
- Acute severe bronchospasm in conjunction with adrenergic bronchodilators.

Neuromuscular disorders and kyphoscoliosis are highlighted because they involve decreased lung volumes.

---

## Topic 3: IPPB Role, Contraindications, and Disadvantages

**Role of IPPB:**

- IPPB has not been shown to provide benefits greater than lung expansion techniques.
- IPPB should be used only as a last resort when all other lung expansion therapies have failed.

**Contraindications:**

- Pneumothorax.
- Intracranial pressure greater than 15 mmHg.
- Nausea.
- Radiographic evidence of bleb.
- Active hemoptysis.
- Recent facial, oral, esophageal, or skull surgery.
- Active, untreated tuberculosis.

**Disadvantages:**

- Mechanical effects are short-term.
- Effects last approximately 15 minutes per treatment.
- IPPB can aerosolize medication.
- IPPB delivers 32% less medication than a simple handheld nebulizer.

**Devices listed in the source:**

- Bird Mark 7.
- PR-II.
- Vortran.

## Topic 4: Administering IPPB - Setup

**Procedure setup:**

- Introduce yourself to the patient and explain the procedure.
- Position the patient comfortably.
- Sitting or semi-Fowler position may be used.
- Determine the appropriate interface: mouthpiece or mask.

**Mask interface:**

- Apply the mask snugly over the nose and mouth.

**Mouthpiece interface:**

- Patient seals lips around the mouthpiece.

**Leak control and coaching:**

- Use nose clips to prevent leaks and improve seal.
- Instruct patient to relax.
- Instruct patient to offer no resistance to lung inflation.

---

## Topic 5: Administering IPPB - Breath Sequence

**Inspiratory phase:**

- Instruct the patient to breathe in.
- This triggers the machine.
- The machine delivers the pressurized breath.

**Expiratory phase:**

- When the lungs fill to maximum capacity, the patient exerts slight resistance.
- This cycles off flow.
- Cycling off flow ends the pressurized breath.

**Between breaths:**

- Patient exhales passively.
- Patient rests for a short period.
- Patient initiates the next breath.
- Encourage the patient to cough as needed.

---

## Topic 6: IPPB Settings

**Inspiratory sensitivity:**

- Measured in negative cmH2O.
- Determines the amount of effort required to trigger or start the machine breath.
- Adjust sensitivity so the patient can trigger the machine without difficulty.

**Inspiratory pressure:**

- Initial peak inspiratory pressure: 15 to 20 cmH2O.
- Adjust pressure up or down for effect and patient comfort.
- Determines inspiratory volume and lung expansion.
- Inspiration cycles off when inspiratory pressure is met.

**Flow rate:**

- Initial flow rate: 30 L/min.
- Determines speed of gas flow during inhalation.
- Adjust flow to provide inspiratory time between 2 and 5 seconds.
- Adjust according to patient comfort.
- Slow flow means more time to reach peak pressure.
- Fast flow means less time to reach peak pressure.
- Flow rate affects inspiratory time.

## Topic 7: Incentive Spirometry and SMI

- Incentive spirometry is abbreviated IS.
- IS facilitates sustained maximal inspiration.
- It uses visual indicators of inspiratory effort.
- Visual indicators help coach patients on optimal performance.
- Visual feedback allows patients to monitor their own effort.

**Sustained maximal inspiration:**

- Abbreviated SMI.
- Slow, deep inspiration from functional residual capacity to total lung capacity.
- Followed by a breath hold of at least 5 seconds.

---

## Topic 8: Incentive Spirometry Indications and Contraindications

**Indications:**

- Prevent atelectasis.
- Minimize atelectasis.
- Resolve atelectasis.
- Postoperative thoracic surgical patients.
- Postoperative abdominal surgical patients.
- Postoperative non-ambulatory patients predisposed to atelectasis.

**Contraindication:**

- Uncooperative patient.
- Patient unable to understand or perform the maneuver.

**Consider other therapy when the patient cannot perform IS:**

- IPPB.
- EzPAP.
- IPV.

---

## Topic 9: Incentive Spirometry Procedure

**Pre-procedure:**

- Review chest X-ray if available.
- Provide proper patient interface: mouth or trach.
- Perform physical assessment.
- Assemble device.
- Set target volume per device insert.
- Position patient for procedure.

**Positioning:**

- High Fowler's: 90 degrees.
- Semi-Fowler's: 30 to 45 degrees.

**Procedure:**

- Set goal marker using package insert.
- Source lists VC greater than 10 mg/kg and IC greater than 15 mg/kg.
- Instruct patient to exhale all air possible from lungs.
- Patient performs SMI to achieve the deepest breath possible.
- Inspiratory time should be 5 to 15 seconds.
- At end inspiration, patient holds breath 2 to 3 seconds.
- Encourage patient to cough forcefully between breaths.
- Coughing helps mobilize and expel secretions.

## Topic 10: High-Yield Review

- IPPB is short-duration lung expansion therapy.
- IPPB is a last resort when other lung expansion therapies have failed.
- IPPB contraindications include pneumothorax, ICP greater than 15 mmHg, nausea, bleb on radiograph, active hemoptysis, recent facial/oral/esophageal/skull surgery, and active untreated tuberculosis.
- Initial IPPB PIP is 15 to 20 cmH2O.
- Initial IPPB flow is 30 L/min.
- IPPB flow is adjusted for an inspiratory time of 2 to 5 seconds.
- IS facilitates SMI using visual indicators.
- SMI is a slow deep inspiration from FRC to TLC followed by at least a 5-second breath hold.
- IS is used to prevent, minimize, or resolve atelectasis.
