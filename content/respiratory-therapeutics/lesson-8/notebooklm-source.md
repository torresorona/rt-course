## Topic 2: Airway Clearance Therapy Modalities

Therapy modalities listed in the source:

- Postural drainage (PD).
- Chest percussion therapy (CPT) and chest vibration.
- Manual percussion by hand.
- Mechanical percussion or pneumatic percussion.
- Positive expiratory pressure (PEP) with vibration.
- Positive expiratory pressure without vibration.
- Intrapulmonary percussive ventilation (IPV).
- High-frequency chest wall oscillation (HFCWO).

---

## Topic 3: Indications for Airway Clearance

**Acute conditions:**

- Abnormalities caused by unilateral lung disease.
- Copious secretions, meaning accumulation greater than normal.
- Acute lobar atelectasis with V/Q mismatch from mucus plugging.
- Acute respiratory failure with retained secretions.

**Chronic conditions:**

- Cystic fibrosis.
- Bronchiectasis.
- Chronic bronchitis.
- Ciliary dyskinetic syndromes.

**Disorders associated with retained secretions:**

- Acute disease.
- Immobile patients.
- Postoperative patients affected by general anesthetics, opiates, and narcotics.
- Inadequate humidification with infiltrates visible on chest radiograph.

View chest X-ray for appropriateness of the airway clearance order.

---

## Topic 4: Chest Physiotherapy Goals

Chest physiotherapy goals:

- Reduce and manage accumulation of pulmonary secretions.
- Decrease airway resistance.
- Improve mobilization and removal of retained secretions.
- Improve ventilation-perfusion distribution.
- Prevent or treat atelectasis.

---

## Topic 5: Postural Drainage

- Postural drainage uses gravity to enhance mobilization of secretions within the airways.
- Secretions gravitate from small airways toward larger airways.
- Body positioning is critical to direct secretion movement.
- Affected areas must be positioned above large airways.
- CPT may be performed alone or with postural drainage.
- Postural drainage may be performed alone or with CPT.

---

## Topic 6: Percussion

- Percussion improves mobilization of pulmonary secretions.

**Procedure:**

- Manual percussion physically strikes chest wall, back, or ribs with cupped hands.
- Mechanical or pneumatic percussion may be applied on the back or chest wall.
- Technique is always performed with the patient in an appropriate postural drainage position.
- Apply folded towel over percussion area to prevent pain.
- Patient periodically performs a strong, effective cough.

---

## Topic 7: Vibration

**Manual vibration:**

- Place crossed hands over an affected lung segment.
- Apply rapid shaking motion with moderate pressure.

**Manual vibration procedure:**

- Place patient in an appropriate postural drainage position as tolerated.
- Instruct patient to breathe in deep.
- Manually apply vibration upon exhalation.
- Repeat several times for at least 5 minutes.
- Direct patient to deep breathe and cough.

**Electromechanical vibration:**

- Perform technique same as pneumatic percussor.
- Apply towel over vibration area for comfort and to prevent irritation.
- Patient performs a strong, effective cough periodically.

---

## Topic 8: Positive Expiratory Pressure Therapy

**Threshold expiratory resistance devices:**

- Provide resistance by set threshold on exhalation only.
- Vibration is not incorporated.
- Source describes this as lung expansion therapy.
- Leads to mobilization of secretions.
- Examples listed: EzPAP and Resistex.

**Vibratory expiratory resistance devices:**

- Produce resistance and vibration.
- Vibration transmits through the airways.
- Vibration shakes loose pulmonary secretions.
- Examples listed: Flutter Valve, Acapella, Quake, and Aerobika.

---

## Topic 9: Expiratory Resistance and Vibration Procedure

**Goal:**

- Splint airways open.
- Enhance mobilization of secretions.

**Procedure:**

- Patient inhales slowly.
- Patient fills lungs to maximum capacity.
- Patient performs breath hold for 3 seconds.
- Patient exhales steadily.
- Exhalation produces sustained vibration to loosen secretions.
- Technique is repeated several times up to 15 minutes.
- Productive coughing follows.

**Additional source points:**

- Procedure may be done with or without postural drainage.
- SVN or MDI often precedes this intervention.
- Should not be done while in Trendelenburg position for patients with elevated intracranial pressure or increased risk for stroke.

---

## Topic 10: Flutter Valve

- Flutter Valve is an expiratory resistance and vibration device.
- It is pipe-shaped.
- Produces positive expiratory pressure range between 10 and 25 cmH2O.
- Goal is for respiratory tract secretions to be shaken loose and mobilized.
- A metal ball vibrates within the device.

## Topic 11: Intrapulmonary Percussive Ventilation

- Intrapulmonary percussive ventilation is abbreviated IPV.
- IPV augments the patient's own spontaneous breathing pattern.
- Patient breathes pressurized, pulsating, dense aerosolized gas into the lungs.

**Mechanism:**

- Pulsatile flow and high-rate delivery of sub-tidal volume gas expands airways.
- Mucus breaks loose from airway walls.
- Mucus moves toward large airways.
- Dense aerosol, saline or medication, is delivered simultaneously to airways.
- Dense aerosol reduces adhesive and cohesive forces of retained secretions.
- IPV enables patient to cough, mobilize, and expectorate secretions.

**Bi-phasic IPV:**

- Uses pulsating bursts of gas at controlled breath rates and pressure.
- Bi-phasic gas flow delivers oxygen to the alveoli.
- Bi-phasic gas flow convectively flushes CO2 out of lung.
- Mucus mobilizes from peripheral to large airways.
- Result is productive patient cough and improved airway clearance.

---

## Topic 12: IPV Interfaces and Treatment Numbers

**Therapy interfaces or settings listed:**

- Patient controlled.
- Inpatient hospital setting.
- Outpatient/home therapy for CF patients.
- Ventilator use in the intensive care unit.
- Thoracic surgery.
- Multiple rib injuries.
- Burn injuries.

**Treatment details:**

- Treatment may use 20 mL medication.
- Treatment may use 20 mL bland aerosol.
- A micro particle nebulizer delivers dense, high-volume, high-efficiency aerosol medications and humidification with percussion.
- Respiratory rates delivered range from 100 to 300 breaths per minute.
- Average respiratory rate is 180 breaths per minute.
- Delivered breaths are sub-tidal volumes.
- Minimum treatment time is 20 minutes.

## Topic 13: High-Frequency Chest Wall Oscillation

- High-frequency chest wall oscillation is abbreviated HFCWO.
- HFCWO is vest therapy.
- The vest is inflatable and non-compliant.
- It fits over the upper torso down to the iliac crest.

**Three-part system:**

- Inflatable, non-stretchable vest.
- Variable pulse generator, or air compressor.
- Pressure hoses connecting the vest to the pulse generator.

**Mechanism:**

- Pulse generator transmits pressure pulses to the chest wall.
- Pressure pulses loosen mucus.
- Mucus mobilizes into larger airways for expectoration.

---

## Topic 14: HFCWO Treatment Administration

**Treatment frequency:**

- Administered twice or more daily.
- Minimum time is 30 minutes.

**Administration:**

- Connect pressure hoses between air pulse generator and patient vest.
- Adjust vest pressure and frequency for effect and patient comfort.
- Pressure range: 25 to 40 mmHg.
- Frequency range: 5 to 25 Hz.

**Frequency conversion:**

- 1 Hz equals 60 cycles/min.
- 5 Hz equals 300 cycles/min.
- 25 Hz equals 1500 cycles/min.

**Additional source points:**

- Effectiveness may be enhanced with proper use of a PEP device during oscillation.
- Vest therapy is managed independently by the patient at home.

## Topic 15: High-Yield Review

- Airway clearance is indicated for retained secretions, atelectasis from mucus plugging, and selected acute or chronic secretion disorders listed in the source.
- Chest physiotherapy reduces secretion accumulation and improves secretion mobilization.
- Postural drainage uses gravity and requires affected areas above large airways.
- Percussion uses cupped hands or mechanical/pneumatic percussion with the patient in an appropriate postural drainage position.
- Manual vibration is applied during exhalation for at least 5 minutes.
- Threshold PEP gives resistance on exhalation only without vibration.
- Vibratory PEP gives resistance plus vibration.
- Flutter Valve PEP range is 10 to 25 cmH2O.
- IPV uses pressurized, pulsating, dense aerosolized gas and sub-tidal volumes.
- IPV rates range from 100 to 300 breaths/min and average 180 breaths/min.
- IPV minimum treatment time is 20 minutes.
- HFCWO minimum time is 30 minutes.
- HFCWO pressure is 25 to 40 mmHg.
- HFCWO frequency is 5 to 25 Hz.
