## Topic 1: Unit Objectives

This unit focuses on three objectives:

- Identify common medical abbreviations.
- Identify elements contained in patient records.
- Identify the purpose for EMR documentation.

The big idea is that respiratory therapists do not just perform therapy. They also document assessment findings, treatments, patient responses, education, and safety-related details in the medical record.

---

## Topic 2: Common Medical Abbreviations

Awake-hour treatment schedules include:

- **BID** means 2 times daily: **08:00 and 20:00**.
- **TID** means 3 times daily: **08:00, 14:00, and 20:00**.
- **QID** means 4 times daily: **08:00, 14:00, 20:00, and 00:00**.

Other common timing abbreviations:

- **prn** means as needed.
- **q.h.** means every hour.
- **q2h** means every 2 hours, such as **08, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18**, and so on.
- **q4h** means every 4 hours, such as **08, 12, 16, 20, 00**, and so on.
- **q6h** means every 6 hours, such as **08, 14, 20, and 02**.

High-yield distinction: q2h, q4h, and q6h are 24-hour schedule patterns. BID, TID, and QID in this unit are presented as awake-hour schedules.

## Topic 3: What Medical Record Documentation Includes

Medical record documentation includes:

- Common clinical data.
- Physical complaints.
- Patient assessments.
- Treatments and medications.
- Care plans and outcomes.
- Limitations placed on the extent of care provided in case of cardiac or respiratory arrest.

Advanced directives listed in the source include:

- **DNR**
- **DNI**
- **AND**

## Topic 4: Elements of a Patient Medical Record

Elements of a patient medical record include:

- Admissions sheets and demographics.
- Physician orders.
- History and physical.
- Laboratory records.
- Reports of radiological procedures.
- Progress notes.
- Medication Administration Record, or **MAR**.
- Records and reports of surgical procedures.
- Discharge plan summary.

The Joint Commission audits the quality and thoroughness of medical record documentation.

---

## Topic 5: Essential Documentation Components

Essential documentation components are:

- **Consistent**
- **Current**
- **Complete**

Careless or insufficient documentation by a provider can result in:

- Misinterpretation.
- Harm to patients.
- Penalties, lawsuits, and negative financial consequences.

Reinforce this as a patient-safety concept. Documentation is not extra work at the end of care. It is part of safe care.

## Topic 6: Common Respiratory Documentation Elements

Respiratory documentation commonly includes:

- Pulmonary medical history and smoking history.
- Vital signs:
  - Heart rate.
  - Respiratory rate.
  - Pulse oximetry.
  - Temperature.
  - Blood pressure.
- Breath sounds.
- Cough effort:
  - Strong or weak.
  - Productive or non-productive.
- Sputum:
  - Consistency.
  - Volume.
  - Color.
- Physical examination of the head, neck, and thorax.
- Blood gas results, or **ABGs**.
- Pulmonary function testing results, or **PFTs**.
- Medication Administration Record, or **MAR**.

Therapeutic interventions that may be documented:

- Lung inflation therapy.
- Bronchial hygiene.
- Ventilator management and modalities.

Other documentation elements:

- Adverse reactions to therapy or medications and the responsive action taken.
- Patient education administered.
- Discharge planning.

## Topic 7: Example Treatment Documentation

Example physician order:

- Patient: Harley Davidson.
- Oxygen to maintain SpO2 above 92%.
- 2.5 mg Albuterol, q4h with peak flows.

Example treatment note:

On **23 Dec 17 at 0800**, Patient Davidson was given **2.5 mg Albuterol** with aerosol mask as ordered, with no adverse reaction.

Documented assessment and response details:

- **BBS:** CTA upper lobes; rhonchi to bases bilaterally, pre- and post-treatment.
- Strong productive cough.
- Sputum: yellow, thick, small volume.
- SpO2: 100% pre- and post-treatment.
- Heart rate: 86/91.
- Respiratory rate: 12/14.
- Patient complaint: nonsmoker, no history of asthma, shortness of breath, wants to stop Albuterol treatment due to tremors.

Use this example to show that a complete treatment note includes the order, therapy delivered, patient response, breath sounds, cough, sputum, oxygenation, vital signs, and patient complaint.

## Topic 8: Physician Orders

Physician orders are:

- Required for all patient care.
- Accurately recorded to assure appropriate care and patient safety.

Types of physician orders:

- Written.
- Verbal.
- Telephone.

The Joint Commission specifies documentation standards for all aspects of patient charts, including physician orders.

---

## Topic 9: Verbal Physician Orders

Verbal orders may be accepted and transcribed by authorized individuals.

Authorized individuals are:

- Medical staff identified by in-hospital policies and procedures.
- Respiratory therapists meet this requirement.

Verbal physician orders must be complete and include:

- Date.
- Time.
- Physician signature.

Verbal and telephone orders are countersigned within **24 hours** as specified by The Joint Commission.

When computer charting is not available, orders should be printed rather than written in cursive.

Goal: eliminate misunderstandings, prevent mistakes, and provide a safe and effective health care environment.

Respiratory therapists must review and understand hospital policies and procedures before receiving and recording verbal orders.

---

## Topic 10: Verify Physician Orders — The Five Rights

The Five Rights help eliminate misunderstandings, prevent mistakes, and provide a safe and effective health care environment.

The Five Rights are:

1. **Patient** — verify the correct patient using **2 identifiers**.
2. **Medication** — examples include Albuterol, Xopenex, and Atrovent.
3. **Modality** — delivery system, such as **SVN**, **MDI**, or **DPI**.
4. **Dose** — volume to dispense.
5. **Time and date** — therapy schedule.

Dose examples:

- **SVN:** 0.5 mg or 2.5 mg.
- **MDI:** 2 puffs.
- **DPI:** 1 inhalation.

High-yield recall: patient, medication, modality, dose, time and date.

## Topic 11: Electronic Medical Records

Computerized medical systems track and record patient information electronically.

Advantages of EMR:

- Electronic capture of information speeds entry.
- Electronic capture improves storage and retrieval of patient information.
- Patient data is stored electronically so authorized health care providers can access the information from any hospital computer or within the extended network.

The EMR can be designed to provide the clinician with a list of activities and choices.

EMR systems are designed to guide clinicians through charting medical care from start to finish.

Some fields are designed with **hard stops**.

Hard stops must be completed before proceeding to the next entry point. They require the clinician to complete an important documentation entry before committing information to the patient's permanent record.

EMR systems may use electronic and paper records.
