## Topic 2: Aerosol Particle Size

- Deposition site in the respiratory tract varies with particle mass produced by nebulizers and inspiratory flow.
- **MMAD:** mass median aerodynamic diameter; average particle size with respect to measure of central tendency.
- Medical gas particles are **heterodispersed**, meaning aerosol particles vary in size.
- **Coalescence:** particles combine and impact size.
- Increased particle mass increases deposition and sedimentation.

## Topic 3: Drug Delivery Devices

Devices in this lesson:

- Metered dose inhaler (MDI).
- Pneumatic jet nebulizer, also called small volume nebulizer (SVN) or handheld nebulizer (HHN).
- Dry powder inhaler (DPI).
- Small particle aerosol generator (SPAG).

---

## Topic 4: Metered Dose Inhaler

- MDI is a small portable aerosol device.
- It is hand-operated.
- It is powered by an HFA propellant.
- Each activation delivers a pre-determined dose in mcg.
- New MDIs include dose-counting devices.

**Valved holding chambers/spacers:**

- Slow aerosol particle velocity.
- Help maintain particles within the optimal respirable size range.
- Reduce coordination required for optimal drug delivery.
- Increase medication quantity delivered with each dose.
- Provide the best respirable dose particle size compared with MDI use without a spacer.
- Spacers should always be used with MDIs.

**Spacer instruction:**

- Shake inhaler thoroughly and place it in spacer/VHC.
- Hold canister vertically.
- Patient sits or stands straight up.
- Exhale completely.
- Place mouthpiece between teeth.
- Actuate MDI at beginning of inhalation.
- Inhale slowly and prevent spacer whistle.
- Withdraw mouthpiece and hold breath for 10 seconds or longer.
- Wait at least 30 seconds between breath actuations.

## Topic 5: Pneumatic Jet Nebulizers

- Deliver a wide variety of inhaled medications.
- Require an external compressed gas source.
- Small volume/handheld nebulizer with reservoir tube or mask is the cheapest and most widely used nebulizer.

**Aerosol formation:**

- High-pressure jet stream creates low pressure lateral to the capillary tube.
- Low pressure pulls fluid up the capillary tube.
- Fluid is drawn into the jet stream and driven into the baffle.
- Baffle creates particulate mist/aerosol small enough to reach lower airways during inhalation.

---

## Topic 6: BEN and BAN

**Breath-enhanced jet nebulizer (BEN):**

- More aerosol released during inspiration.
- Two one-way valves.
- Prevents aerosol lost to environment.
- Nebulized aerosol is contained until patient inhales.
- Upon inspiration, one-way valve opens and directs aerosol from reservoir to patient airway.
- Examples: Pari LC Plus and NebuTech.
- Higher viscosity solutions such as gentamicin require gas flow rates of 10 to 12 L/min to produce suitable aerosol particles.

**Breath-actuated nebulizer (BAN):**

- Two settings: inspiration only/breath actuation and continuous.
- Eliminates waste because nebulization stops during exhalation.
- Increases delivered dose more than three times compared with continuous nebulizers.

## Topic 7: Administration of SVN

**Before therapy:**

- Verify physician order and review patient chart.
- Introduce yourself and explain purpose for visit.
- Provide therapy instructions.
- Answer questions to affect understanding and compliance.
- Assemble flowmeter, "Xmas tree adaptor", small bore tubing, and interface: mask, trach collar, or mouthpiece.
- Label therapy devices with patient label and equipment change date according to local policy, usually every 48 hours.

**Treatment:**

- Seat patient upright if possible.
- Perform physical assessment.
- Auscultate breath sounds, cough effort, and sputum production.
- Measure heart rate and respiratory rate.
- Measure SpO2.
- Perform peak flow measurement.
- Add medication and adjust gas flow to 6 L/min or greater.
- Patient breathes normally with occasional slow deep breaths and 2 to 3 second breath holds.
- Hold nebulizer upright.
- Continue until aerosol is no longer produced or sputtering occurs.

**Post treatment:**

- Rinse nebulizer with sterile or distilled water and allow it to air dry.
- Store equipment in clean patient bag at bedside.
- Reassess patient including post peak flow.
- Document therapy the same as MDI administration.

## Topic 8: Mechanical Ventilator Medication Delivery

- Respiratory medications are delivered via adapters on the inspiratory limb of the ventilator circuit before the circuit wye.
- Nebulizer adapters or HHN may be used.
- MDI adapters or collapsible holding chamber may be used.
- DPIs cannot deliver medication to mechanically ventilated patients.

---

## Topic 9: Dry Powder Inhaler

- Uses micronized drug particles.
- No propellants.
- Patient must actively inhale through the device to suspend drug particles.
- Delivers the most medication to lung compared with other devices.

**DPI instructions:**

- Check correct medicine.
- Remove inhaler cap.
- Add or load a dose.
- Exhale completely.
- Do not exhale into inhaler.
- Exhaling into inhaler can blow powder out.
- Moist exhaled air can cause powder to clump and clog inhaler.
- Place inhaler in mouth and form tight seal.
- Inhale quickly and deeply through 2 or 3 seconds.
- Hold breath for 10 seconds.

---

## Topic 10: SPAG

- Small particle aerosol generator.
- Used to deliver Ribavirin (Virazole), an antiviral drug.
- Treatment indications: Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) and Parainfluenza Virus (PIV).

---

## Topic 11: High-Yield Review

- Spacers should always be used with MDIs.
- SVN/HHN is the cheapest and most widely used nebulizer.
- BEN contains aerosol until inspiration.
- BAN stops nebulization during exhalation.
- SVN gas flow: 6 L/min or greater.
- DPI requires active inhalation and cannot be used for mechanically ventilated patients.
- SPAG delivers Ribavirin for RSV and PIV.
