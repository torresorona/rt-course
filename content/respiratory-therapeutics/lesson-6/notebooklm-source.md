## Topic 2: Normal Airway Clearance and Cough

Normal airway clearance requirements:

- Patent airway.
- Functional mucociliary escalator.
- Strong effective cough.

Four phases of normal cough:

- Irritation.
- Inspiration.
- Compression.
- Expulsion.

## Topic 3: Purpose of Controlled Breathing

Controlled breathing techniques are used to:

- Expand the lungs.
- Mobilize secretions.
- Support airway clearance.
- Reduce and/or reverse breathing difficulties.

These techniques are used for specific control of breathing and coughing. They have specific applications and usefulness according to patient limitations and capabilities.

## Topic 4: Directed Cough Techniques

Directed cough techniques:

- Manually assisted coughing.
- Forced expiratory maneuver.
- Autogenic drainage.

Indications for directed cough:

- Aid removal of retained secretions from central airways.
- Presence of atelectasis.
- Prophylaxis against postoperative pulmonary complications.
- Routine part of bronchial hygiene in patients with CF, bronchiectasis, chronic bronchitis, necrotizing pulmonary infection, or spinal cord injury.
- Integral part of other bronchial hygiene therapies, such as postural drainage therapy, PEP therapy, and incentive spirometry.
- Obtain sputum specimens for diagnostic analysis.

## Topic 5: Manually Assisted Cough

- Performed with physical manipulation of chest or abdomen by medical assistant or use of mechanical device.

**Heimlich maneuver:**

- Patient sits at 45 degrees facing away from assister.
- Patient inhales as deeply as possible.
- Abdomen is forced in and upward with closed fist by medical assister.
- This simulates a forceful cough.

**Recumbent method:**

- Patient lies supine.
- After deep inhalation, patient's abdomen is pushed inward and upward with overlapping palms.
- This simulates a forceful cough during exhalation.

**Cough Assist:**

- Cough Assist, or insufflator-exsufflator device, is listed as an option.

## Topic 6: Forced Expiratory Technique

- Forced expiratory technique is also called huff cough.
- Consists of one or two forced expirations of middle to low lung volume with open glottis.
- Followed by a period of diaphragmatic breathing and relaxation.

**Procedure:**

- Patient takes breaths of varying depth.
- Exhalation is forceful with huffing to assist keeping glottis open.
- During exhalation, patient remains conscious of effort to push secretions toward upper airway for expectoration when raised.

## Topic 7: Autogenic Drainage

- Airway clearance technique characterized by breathing control.
- Patient focuses on rate, depth, and location of lung volumes during respiration.
- Patient uses diaphragmatic breathing to mobilize secretions.
- Patient should be sitting for maximum benefit.

Three phases, each approximately 3 minutes:

- **Unsticking phase:** begin breathing at low lung volumes with inspiratory breath holds, followed by controlled exhalation to prevent airway collapse.
- **Collecting phase:** patient progressively increases inspired volumes and expiratory airflow.
- **Evacuation phase:** coughing is suppressed until all phases are completed.

**Procedure:**

- Patient breathes in at variable depths.
- Starts with shallow inhalations and progresses to deeper inhalations.
- Exhalation size/depth matches inhalation size/depth.
- Phase 1: unsticking mucus with rapid and shallow breaths.
- Phase 2: collecting mucus from small to large airways.
- Phase 3: mucus evacuation; mucus progresses toward large airways and is expelled.

## Topic 8: Pursed-Lips Breathing

- During exhalation, lips are pursed to create expiratory resistance and back pressure.
- This splints airways open and helps prevent airway collapse and air trapping.
- Prolongs the expiratory phase of breathing.
- Primarily used in patients with severe COPD.
- Helps reduce or alleviate dyspnea.
- More effectively empties lungs at end of each breath.

**Procedure:**

- Patient inhales comfortably through mouth or nose at normal pace.
- Patient exhales through pursed, whistle-position lips at twice or greater the duration of inhalation.
- Source lists this as 1:4.
- Reduces trapped air in patient lungs.

**Benefits:**

- Splints airways open to increase ease of exhalation.
- Reduces air trapping, also called dynamic hyperinflation.
- Prolongs exhalation time to increase CO2 elimination.
- Improves rate of exhalation to reduce WOB.
- Improves ventilation and reduces dyspnea.

## Topic 9: Diaphragmatic Breathing

- Breathing technique intended to help reengage and strengthen diaphragm while breathing.
- Strengthens diaphragm.
- Decreases WOB.
- Decreases oxygen demand.
- Uses less effort and energy to breathe.
- Uses lower abdominal muscles to assist ventilation and reduce WOB.
- Patients with COPD have hyperinflated chest and flattened diaphragm, causing use of upper chest accessory muscles.

**Procedure:**

- Performed lying or sitting.
- Shoulders relaxed.
- One hand on chest and one hand on stomach.
- Inhale through nose for 2 seconds.
- Focus on moving stomach outward more than chest.
- Encourage slow exhalation and incorporate pursed-lips breathing.
- Repeat exercise as able.
