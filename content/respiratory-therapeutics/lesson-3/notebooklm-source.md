## Topic 2: Indications for Oxygen

**Documented hypoxemia (ABG confirmed):**

- PaO2 less than **60 mmHg** or SaO2 less than **90%** on room air (**21% FiO2**).
- PaO2 or SaO2 below desirable range for specific situations (e.g., pneumonia).

**Acute care when hypoxemia is suspected:**

- Cor pulmonale.
- Congestive heart failure.
- Severe trauma (severe blood loss).
- Acute myocardial infarction.

**Short-term therapy:**

- Post-anesthesia recovery.

---

## Topic 3: Contraindications and Hazards

**Contraindications:**

- Few specific contraindications to O2 when indications are present.
- Device contraindication: nasal cannulas and nasopharyngeal catheters in **pediatric and neonatal patients with obstruction**.

**General hazards:**

- PaO2 ≥ 60 mmHg: rare ventilatory depression in COPD with elevated PaCO2.
- FiO2 > 0.5: absorption atelectasis, O2 toxicity, depressed ciliary/leukocyte function.
- Worsened lung injury with paraquat or bleomycin.
- Laser bronchoscopy/tracheostomy: use minimal FiO2 to avoid intratracheal ignition.
- Increased fire hazard with high FiO2.
- Bacterial contamination with nebulizers/humidifiers.

**Infant hazards:**

- Premature infants: PaO2 > 80 mmHg → retinopathy of prematurity (ROP).
- Duct-dependent congenital heart lesions: high PaO2 can upset pulmonary/systemic balance.
- O2 flow at face may alter respiratory pattern.

## Topic 4: Low-Flow vs High-Flow

| Type | FiO2 | Flow |
|------|------|------|
| Low-flow | Variable | Generally < 10 L/min |
| High-flow | Fixed | Generally > 10 L/min |

**Low-flow FiO2 depends on:**

- Tidal volume and RR (minute volume).
- Increased Vt or RR → decreased delivered FiO2.
- Inspiratory flow rate.
- Air entrainment (more entrainment → lower FiO2).

**Flow deficit example:**

- Inspiratory flow **20 L/min**, device **8 L/min** → deficit **12 L/min** from room air.

## Topic 5: Low-Flow Devices

### Nasal catheter

- Standard until late 1960s; soft PVC ~12 inches with distal holes.
- Adult: **12 and 14 Fr**; pediatric: **8 and 10 Fr**.
- Flow **1–5 L/min**; FiO2 **22%–35%**.
- Contraindications: septum deviation, mucosal congestion, nasal polyps.
- Replaced by nasal cannula.

### Nasal cannula

- Most widely used low-flow device.
- Flow **0–6 L/min**; humidify above **4 L/min**.

**Rule of Fours:**

```text
20% + (O2 L/min x 4) = estimated FiO2
```

| L/min | FiO2 |
|-------|------|
| 1 | 24% |
| 2 | 28% |
| 3 | 32% |
| 4 | 36% |
| 5 | 40% |
| 6 | 44% |

### Simple mask

- Flow **5–10 L/min**; FiO2 **30%–60%**; humidified up to **15 L/min**.
- Run **> 5 L/min** to wash out CO2.

### Non-rebreathing mask (NRB)

- Reservoir + one-way valves; FiO2 **60%–80%**.
- Flow **10 to >15 L/min**; reservoir inflated **> 50%**.
- **Only device for Heliox**; not humidified.

### Trans-tracheal catheter

- Flow **< 6 L/min**; FiO2 **22%–35%**; long-term O2.
- Hazards: site infection, mucus plugging.

## Topic 6: High-Flow Devices — LVN

- FiO2 **30%–100%**; delivers aerosolized water; large bore tubing.
- Heated: hot plate, doughnut/yoke collar, immersion heater.
- Interfaces: aerosol mask, face tent (post-extubation), Briggs T-piece (trach/weaning), trach collar.

---

## Topic 7: Venturi Mask

- FiO2 **24%–60%**; total flow can exceed **100 L/min**.
- If total flow < inspiratory demand → delivered FiO2 decreases.
- Occluding ports ↑ FiO2, ↓ total flow — do not obstruct ports.
- COPD often needs FiO2 **24%–35%**.

**Entrainment ratios:**

| FiO2 | Air : O2 |
|------|----------|
| 0.24 | 25:1 |
| 0.28 | 10:1 |
| 0.30 | 8:1 |
| 0.35 | 5:1 |
| 0.40 | 3:1 |
| 0.50 | 1.7:1 |
| 0.60 | 1:1 |

**Formula:**

```text
(100 - % O2) : (% O2 - 21)
```

Examples: 24% → 25:1; 40% → 3:1. Max calculation **60%**.

## Topic 8: HFNC (Vapotherm)

- FiO2 **21%–100%**.
- Adults **5–40 L/min**; neonates/children **1–8 L/min**.
- Heated humidity; prongs < half nares diameter.
- Effects: CO2 washout, MAP, WOB, humidification, mucous clearance.

---

## Topic 9: Oxygen Analyzers

- Required when exact FiO2 is critical.
- **2-point calibration** each use; error **≤ +/- 2%**.

**Galvanic fuel cell:** slow (<60 sec), no batteries, consumes O2 continuously.

**Polarographic (Clark):** faster (<30 sec), battery powered, intermittent.

**Calibration:**

1. Connect cell; verify alarms.
2. Calibrate **21%** (room air) and **100%** (bag with O2).
3. Set high/low alarms.
4. In-line with Briggs adaptor; ventilator: pre-heater/humidifier; recalibrate for altitude; alarms ±10% of FiO2.

## Topic 10: High-Yield Review

- Indications: PaO2 <60 or SaO2 <90% on room air; acute MI, CHF, trauma; post-anesthesia.
- Low-flow: variable FiO2, <10 L/min, Ve affects delivery.
- High-flow: fixed FiO2, total flow exceeds demand.
- Know device flow/FiO2 table and 2-point calibration.
