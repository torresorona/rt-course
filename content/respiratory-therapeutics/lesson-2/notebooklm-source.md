## Topic 2: Compressed Air

Compressed air is a colorless, tasteless, odorless gas that supports life and is non-flammable.

Clinical applications:

- Dilute 100% O2 to produce FiO2 from **22% to 99%** for therapeutic administration.
- Serve as driving gas for devices when oxygen is not desired for that purpose.

Compressed air is produced from filtered ambient air.

---

## Topic 3: Oxygen

Oxygen is a colorless, tasteless, non-flammable gas that is slightly heavier than air at **STPD**.

STPD means:

- Standard temperature: **0 degrees C** or **273 K**.
- Pressure: **760 mmHg**.
- Dry: no water vapor.

Clinical application:

- Driving gas for breathing devices when FiO2 greater than 21% is desired for life support.

Oxygen facts:

- Fractional distillation process: gaseous oxygen becomes liquefied.
- Liquefaction occurs when temperature drops to **-297 degrees F**.
- FiO2 of air is **20.95%** and remains constant to an altitude of 60 miles above mean sea level.
- PO2 varies with barometric pressure: **O2 = Pb x FiO2**.
- Oxygen supports and accelerates the rate of combustion, making fire burn faster and hotter.
- Oxygen cylinder color is **green**.

---

## Topic 4: Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide is a colorless, slight acid-taste, non-flammable gas that does not support combustion or animal life.

Clinical application:

- Clinical research.

Carbon dioxide facts:

- Carbonic acid forms when CO2 is dissolved in water.
- Carbonic acid is corrosive to metals.
- At sea level, CO2 concentration is very low, about **0.03%**.
- CO2 cylinder color is **grey**.

---

## Topic 5: Carbogen

Carbogen is CO2 mixed with oxygen.

Clinical application:

- Carbogen added to mechanically ventilated patients serves as an alternative to adding mechanical dead space.

Two mixtures used in medicine:

- **90% O2 mixed with 10% CO2**.
- **95% O2 mixed with 5% CO2**.

Carbogen cylinder color is **grey/green**.

---

## Topic 6: Helium and Heliox

Helium is:

- Colorless.
- Tasteless.
- Non-flammable.
- An inert gas.
- Does not support life or combustion.
- The second lightest element on the periodic table.
- Extremely rare in the atmosphere, occurring in very small amounts: **0.000524%**.

Helium cylinder color is **brown**.

Heliox is a mixture of helium and oxygen.

Heliox promotes laminar flow within the airways by:

- Lower density.
- Higher viscosity.

Laminar flow is determined with the Reynolds number gas mixture formula:

```text
Re = inertial forces / viscous forces
Re = (v r p) / eta
```

Where:

- **v** = velocity of gas movement.
- **r** = radius of the tube.
- **p** = pressure driving the gas.
- **eta** = viscosity.

Heliox mixtures:

- 80/20.
- 70/30.
- 60/40.

Methods of delivery:

- Non-rebreather mask.
- Non-invasive positive pressure ventilators.
- Mechanical ventilator.

Heliox cylinder color is **brown/green**.

## Topic 7: Heliox Indications and Goals

Resistance to breathing occurs in the upper and large airways, causing large increases in work of breathing. The source notes approximately **80%**.

Upper-airway indications:

- Vocal cord or trachea inflammation.
- Tumors or foreign bodies in the trachea or main stem bronchi.
- Croup.
- Bronchial cancer.
- Large airway obstruction.

Use of a less dense gas, such as a helium-oxygen mixture, can increase medication particle deposition.

Lower-airway indications:

- Acute asthma.
- COPD: ongoing research, promising.

Heliox goals:

- Increased peak flows.
- Decreased respiratory rate.
- Decreased work of breathing.
- Decreased use of accessory muscles.
- Decreased respiratory rate after initiating Heliox.

## Topic 8: Nitric Oxide

Nitric oxide is:

- Colorless.
- Tasteless.
- Has a metallic odor.
- Non-flammable.
- Does not support life.
- Exists in very small amounts in the atmosphere.
- Also referred to as nitrogen monoxide and inhaled nitric oxide.

Nitric oxide is a vasodilator.

Clinical application:

- Treatment of persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn, or **PPHN**.

Non-FDA applications listed:

- Acute and primary pulmonary hypertension in adults.
- Revascularization perfusion after lung transplants.

Dose delivered:

- Parts per million, or **PPM**.

Nitric oxide cylinder color is **teal/black**.

## Topic 9: Storage, Transport, and Delivery of Medical Gas

Medical gas storage methods:

- Liquid storage.
- Compressed cylinders.
- Concentrators.

Most large hospitals use liquid bulk oxygen storage.

Liquid oxygen facts:

- A small volume of liquid O2 equals a large volume of gaseous O2.
- **1 liter of liquid O2 = 860 liters of gaseous O2**.
- Liquid O2 must be stored at **-118 degrees C** or it will revert to gaseous state.

Central gas piping in hospitals incorporates pressure-reducing valves that regulate and maintain outlet pressure at **50 psig**.

Patient room wall outlets supply oxygen and air at **50 psig**.

All respiratory equipment operates at **50 psig** and can be connected directly to wall outlets.

## Topic 10: Compressed Gas Cylinders

Since the 1890s, steel cylinders have been used to store and transport compressed gases. They are still used today to transport patients on oxygen.

U.S. Department of Transportation regulations specify that high-pressure medical gas cylinders be made of seamless construction from:

- High-quality steel.
- Chromium-molybdenum alloy.
- Aluminum.

Cylinders must be tested at regular intervals to ensure they remain safe for filling to their specified pressure.

Testing intervals:

- Steel cylinders: at least once every **10 years**.
- Aluminum cylinders: every **5 years**.

Exterior inspection looks for:

- Dents.
- Rust.
- Corrosion.
- Deep scarring.

Interior inspection is done by pressure testing at least five-thirds greater than the **2015 psig** service pressure, or **3358 psig**.

Cylinder color codes:

- Oxygen: green.
- CO2: grey.
- Carbogen: grey/green.
- Helium: brown.
- Heliox: brown/green.
- Nitric oxide: teal/black.

## Topic 11: Safety Indexed Connector Systems

Safety indexed connector systems ensure the correct gas is delivered to patients using the correct equipment.

Systems listed:

- American Standard Safety System, or **ASSS**.
- Pin Index Safety System, or **PISS**.
- Diameter Index Safety System, or **DISS**.

ASSS:

- Restricts use of regulators attached to large cylinders.
- Applies to large cylinders such as H or K oxygen cylinders.
- Uses nut size and thread design to restrict regulators to the specific gas they are designed for and calibrated to.

PISS:

- Governs attachment of specific gas regulators to small cylinders such as D and E.
- Uses a series of pin placements on the regulator.
- Pin placement must match the pin holes drilled in the yoke of the cylinder.

DISS:

- Uses threaded connectors with unique diameter for each type of gas.
- Prevents erroneous connection.
- Used with low-pressure gas systems less than 300 psi, usually **50 psi** for respiratory and anesthesia equipment.

## Topic 12: Compressed Gas Regulators

Regulators reduce cylinder pressure to **50 psi working pressure**.

Types of regulators:

- Preset.
- Adjustable, uncommon.
- Single stage.
- Multiple stage.

Single-stage regulators:

- Reduce cylinder pressure to **50 psig**.
- Have one safety relief device.
- Commonly used for D and E cylinders.

Multiple-stage regulators:

- Reduce cylinder pressure in 2 or 3 steps.
- Final result is approximately **50 psig**.
- Have multiple safety relief devices.

Attaching a regulator:

1. Obtain the appropriate pressure regulator for the intended tank.
2. PISS tanks require a tank valve key.
3. ASSS tanks require a brass non-spark-producing wrench.
4. Before attaching a regulator, crack open the cylinder valve briefly.
5. The burst of gas removes debris and dust from the outlet valve.
6. Position the regulator on the tank and tighten in place.
7. Turn the tank on and check for leaks.
8. Attach delivery device and put into use.

Newer O2 cylinders are equipped with regulators to reduce pressure and display tank contents in psi.

## Topic 13: Flow Metering Devices

Flow metering devices include:

- Thorpe tube.
- Bourdon gauge.
- Flow restrictor.

Thorpe tube:

- Glass tube with graduated markings indicating flow in L/min.
- Gas entering the flowmeter creates a pressure differential to lift the float.
- Ball floats are read at the center.
- Flat floats are read from the top.
- Affected by gravity, such as when a cylinder is lying on its side.
- Most are scaled from 0 to 16 L/min.

Pressure-compensated Thorpe tube:

- Needle valve is distal to the float.
- Reads accurately with backpressure, such as kinked oxygen tubing.
- If O2 tubing is occluded, the float drops to zero.

Non-compensated Thorpe tube:

- Needle valve is proximal to the float.
- Does not read accurately with backpressure.
- If O2 tubing is occluded, the float remains at the same liter flow.
- Not common.

Confirming compensation:

1. Connect to wall source.
2. Set flow to **5 L/min**.
3. Ball should rise to **5 L/min**.
4. Occlude outlet with a finger.
5. Ball should drop to zero if functioning properly.

Bourdon gauge:

- Can be calibrated to measure pressure or flow.
- Not affected by gravity.
- Accurate if the cylinder is on its side.
- Can be inaccurate with high resistance or occluded O2 tubing, like a non-compensated Thorpe tube.

Flow restrictor:

- Uses a series of holes in a disk calibrated to deliver different flow rates when connected to **50 psi**.

## Topic 14: Air-Oxygen Blender

An air-oxygen blender blends **50 psi air** and **50 psi O2** to deliver a specific FiO2.

Input connections:

- Oxygen: 50 psig.
- Air: 50 psig.

Outlet:

- Blended gas at 50 psig.
- Flowmeters can be attached to deliver specific FiO2.

Typical uses:

- Oxygen delivery devices for pediatric and neonatal patients.
- Mechanical ventilators.

Blender setup:

1. Gather equipment: flowmeter, oxygen hose, air hose, and oxygen blender.
2. Connect oxygen and air hoses for gas service.
3. Use wall gas outlets or tank cylinders.
4. Attach flowmeter to outlet on blender.
5. Connect O2 device to flowmeter.

## Topic 15: Oxygen Cylinder Duration

When a therapist cannot be present while a patient is on an oxygen device powered by cylinders, it is necessary to calculate cylinder duration.

Formula components:

- Cylinder pressure.
- Conversion factor, which converts cylinder pressure to cylinder volume.
- Delivery device liter flow.
- Safety factor, used to leave a reserve amount or time so the therapist can change the tank before it runs empty and the patient no longer receives O2.

Oxygen tank factors:

- D = **0.16 L/psig**.
- E = **0.28 L/psig**.
- G = **2.41 L/psig**.
- H and K = **3.14 L/psig**.

Oxygen cylinder duration formula:

```text
[(Pressure - 500) x tank factor] / flow = duration in minutes
```

Oxygen duration example:

A patient is receiving **5 L/min** via nasal cannula from an E cylinder with **2000 psig**.

```text
[(2000 - 500) x 0.28] / 5
(1500 x 0.28) / 5
420 / 5 = 84 minutes
```

Duration is **84 minutes**, or **1 hour and 24 minutes**.

## Topic 16: Heliox Cylinder Duration

Heliox tank factor or K-factor:

- H-cylinder = **2.50 L/psig**.

Heliox tank duration formula:

```text
(Cylinder pressure x tank factor) / flow = duration in minutes
```

Heliox duration example:

An H tank of Heliox has **2200 psi** and is delivering Heliox via non-rebreather mask at **10 L/min**.

```text
(2200 x 2.50) / 10
5500 liters / 10 L/min = 550 minutes
550 / 60 = 9.17 hours
```

## Topic 17: Heliox Delivery with Oxygen Flowmeters

If an oxygen flowmeter is used to deliver Heliox, the flow delivered will be greater than what the float indicates.

Correction formulas:

- **80/20:** O2 flow x **1.8** = actual flow.
- **70/30:** O2 flow x **1.6** = actual flow.
- **60/40:** O2 flow x **1.4** = actual flow.

Examples:

- 80/20 Heliox at 10 L/min: **10 x 1.8 = 18 L/min**.
- 70/30 Heliox at 10 L/min: **10 x 1.6 = 16 L/min**.
- 60/40 Heliox at 10 L/min: **10 x 1.4 = 14 L/min**.

It is better to use an appropriate Heliox-calibrated flowmeter.

## Topic 18: Oxygen Concentrators

Physical separation methods:

- Molecular sieves.
- Vacuum method.

Molecular sieves:

- Composed of inorganic sodium aluminum silicate pellets.
- Remove nitrogen, carbon dioxide, and water vapor.
- Provide O2 concentrations of **92% to 95%** at flow less than **2 L/min**.
- At greater than **5 L/min**, O2 concentrations range between **85% and 93%**.
- Most common type.

Vacuum method:

- Draws ambient air through a semipermeable plastic membrane.
- Provides O2 concentration of approximately **40%**.
- Not commonly used.
