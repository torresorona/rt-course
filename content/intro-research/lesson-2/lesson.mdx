# Introduction to Research Mock Exam

This mock exam pulls together the core concepts from the Fundamentals of Respiratory Care Research lesson. Use it as a final pass after reviewing the purpose and importance of research, how to review the literature, the steps and feasibility of conducting a study, statistical basics, the structure of a research article, peer review, and the types of research.

## Topics Covered

- **Overview and importance:** becoming an educated consumer of research, finding a mentor, the three missions of academic medicine, and the R.A.C.E. stakeholders.
- **Reviewing the literature:** bibliographic databases and "My NCBI," search engines (PubMed, SearchMedica), synthesized databases (Cochrane), portals (MD Consult, Ovid), electronic journals and books, general internet resources, and bibliographic software.
- **Starting research and feasibility:** device evaluation, the IRB, and the M.A.D.S.A.K. feasibility factors.
- **Steps in research:** developing a study idea, the hypothesis, writing a study protocol, analyzing data (mean and standard deviation, p-value, t-test, ANOVA, chi-square/Fisher Exact), and publishing via abstract, poster, or paper.
- **Article structure and peer review:** the sections of a research article and the peer-review process and outcomes.
- **Types of research:** quantitative, qualitative, descriptive, and analytic.

| Concept | High-yield point |
|---------|------------------|
| **Minimum research expectation** | Become an educated consumer of medical research |
| **Becoming a researcher** | Find a mentor |
| **Three missions of academic medicine** | Heal, teach, discover |
| **R.A.C.E. stakeholders** | Researchers, administrators, clinicians, educators |
| **Common skill** | Read and critically evaluate published scientific reports |
| **Most popular search engine** | PubMed (U.S. National Library of Medicine) |
| **Leading synthesized database** | The Cochrane Collaboration |
| **IRB** | Required for studies of human subjects |
| **Feasibility (M.A.D.S.A.K.)** | Measurability, availability of subjects, duration/timing, significance, availability of equipment/funds, knowledge/experience |
| **Hypothesis** | A supposition or proposed explanation for an observation |
| **T-test / ANOVA / chi-square** | Two means / more than two means / proportions |
| **Ways to present findings** | Abstract, poster, paper |
| **Article structure** | Abstract, introduction, materials and methods, results, discussion, conclusion, references |
| **Quantitative vs. qualitative** | How many vs. why |
| **Descriptive vs. analytic** | Generates hypotheses vs. tests hypotheses |
