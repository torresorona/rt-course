Peak Inspiratory Flow Rate
Changing flow rate can alter the Inspiratory Time (I-Time)
Increasing PIFR decreases I-Time results in 
Increased Expiratory Time (E-Time)
Increased Airway resistance (RAW)
Increased PIP

Decreased PIP

Inspiratory Flow Pattern
Adjustable setting on some ventilators

Inspiratory Time
Normal I-time 0.75 - 1.5 seconds (adults)   
Used in conjunction with Peak Inspiratory Flow (PIFR) to determine a preset Tidal Volume
I-Time X PIFR = Tidal Volume
1Sec x 60Lpm = 1L Vt

Changing I-Time can alter the PIFR and I:E ratio
Increasing (longer I-time) requires lower PIFR to deliver preset Vt and results in 
Decreased E-Time
Decreased RAW
Decreased PIP 
Improved gas distribution 
However long I-Times to  air trapping / Auto PEEP
Decreasing I-Time (shorter I-time) requires higher PIFR to deliver preset Vt and results in 
Increased E-Time
Increased Airway resistance (RAW)
Increased Peak Inspiratory Pressures (PiP) 
Poor gas distribution 


Calculating I-Time
Using cycle time and I:E
I time = Cycle Time ÷ sum of I:E ratio
Cycle time = 60 ÷ Resp rate
Example:
RR - 10b/min (Cycle time = 6sec) 
I:E = 1:2
6 ÷ 3 = 2sec I-Time

Using tidal volume and flow rate
I-Time = Vt (L) / Flow rate (L/min)
.5L ÷ (60L/min ÷ 60) converts L/min to L/Sec
.5L ÷ 1L/sec = .5 second I-time
I:E Ratio
The ratio of I-time to E-time
Normal I:E 
Adult-  1:2 - 1:3
Infant- 1:1
Changed based on disease process
Obstructive disease may need high ratio ex.  1:6 
ARDS may use INVERSED ratio ex 2:1

Ventilator Associated Pneumonia (VAP)
Defined
Pneumonia occurring more than 48-72hr post intubation and mechanical ventilation. 
VAP prevention Protocols
Appropriate hand hygiene
Gentle suctioning 
Place patient in semi-recumbent position (30 – 45 degree)
Do not routinely change ventilator circuits
Drain circuit condensate AWAY from patient
Heated wire circuits / HME
MDI over HHN (if HHN change after every tx)
Ultrasonic nebs are as effective as MDIs 
Sedation holidays when performing spontaneous breathing trials 
Use non-invasive ventilation whenever possible to avoid intubation
Regular oral hygiene (every 4 hrs)

Circuit change
AARC Recommendation #1. Ventilator circuits should not be changed routinely for infection control purposes. The available evidence suggests no patient harm and considerable cost savings associated with extended ventilator circuit change intervals. The maximum duration of time that circuits can be used safely is unknown. 

Additional CDC guidance: Change the circuit when it is visibly soiled or mechanically malfunctioning 

Circuit change
AARC Recommendation #2. Evidence is lacking related to VAP and issues of:
Heated versus unheated circuits 
Type of heated humidifier
Method for filling the humidifier
Technique for clearing condensate from the ventilator circuit. 
It is prudent to avoid excessive accumulation of condensate in the circuit. 
Care should be taken to avoid accidental drainage of condensate into the patient’s airway and to avoid contamination of caregivers during ventilator disconnection or during disposal of condensate. 
Care should be taken to avoid disconnecting the ventilator circuit from the patients airway, which could contaminate the interior of the circuit. 

Circuit change
AARC Recommendation #3. Although the available evidence suggests a lower VAP rate with passive humidification (heat moisture exchanger – HME) than with active humidification (Heated Passover or wick humidifier), other issues related to the use of passive humidifiers (resistance, dead space volume, airway occlusion risk) preclude a recommendation for the general use of these devices. The decision to use a passive humidifier should not be based solely on infection control considerations.

AARC Recommendation #4. Passive humidifiers (HME’s) do not need to be changed daily for reasons of infection control or technical performance. They can be safely used for at least 48 hours, and with some patient populations some devices may be able to be used for up to 1 week.

Circuit change
AARC Recommendation #5. The use of closed suction catheters should be considered part of a VAP prevention strategy. When closed suction catheters are used, they do not need to be changed daily for infection control purposes. The maximum duration of time that closed suction catheters can be used safely is unknown. 

AARC Recommendation #6. Clinicians caring for mechanically ventilated patients should be aware of risk factors for VAP (eg, nebulizer therapy, manual ventilation, and patient transport).

Weaning – The process of decreasing ventilator support (oxygen, PEEP, mandatory rate) and allowing patients to assume a greater portion of their ventilation/WOB.

“Ventilator discontinuation” or “Ventilator Liberation” – process of removing a patient from the ventilator

Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT)
One of several methods to assess a patient's ability to breathe while receiving minimal or no ventilator support.
Performed several times a day interspersed with periods of fully supported mechanical ventilation
Can last minutes to hours but are gradually extended based on patients response
Faster than SIMV for discontinuing ventilatory support 
MUST routinely assess the patient during spontaneous trials 
Successful SBT of should prompt consideration for extubation 

Types of SBT’s

Criteria for failing a SBT 
Sustained respiratory rate greater than 35 breaths/min
Sustained SpO2 less than 90% 
Heart Rate 
Tachycardia (140 bpm) or 20% increase from baseline
bradycardia 20% decrease from baseline 
Blood Pressure
Hypertension – systolic pressure over 180 mmHg
Hypotension – systolic pressure less than 90 mmHg 
Agitation / anxiety
Diaphoresis
Change in mental status

SBT failure
When an SBT is failed, ventilatory support should be promptly reestablished, the reason for failure identified and corrected before another SBT is preformed. 

Patients should be rested for several hours on full ventilator support before attempting another SBT

Determining Brain Death
Clinical Evaluation (By Neurologist/ Critical Care MD):
Absence of the following need to be assessed:
Pupillary reflex (Eyes do not react to light or react unequally)
Corneal reflex (AKA blink reflex, touching the eye or dropping Normal Saline in it)
Gag reflex
Oculovestibular reflex (Introduce iced water into the external ear canal)
Electroencephalogram (EEG)
If these are absent, an apnea test can be performed.
Determining Brain Death
Apnea Test
Apnea test procedure:
Before beginning, a compensated blood gas must be achieved. 
 PaCO2 35-40mmHg
 PaO2 200mmHg

Patient is removed from the ventilator
100% O2 is delivered via a catheter placed at the carina at 4-6Lpm
Watch patient’s chest for spontaneous breathing.
Obtain arterial blood gas after 8-10 mins. (if no chest rise noted)

Apnea Test
Results 
Positive test
PaCO2 increased to 60mmHg or 20mmHg above baseline, 
AND the pt remains apneic 

Return patient to full ventilation
