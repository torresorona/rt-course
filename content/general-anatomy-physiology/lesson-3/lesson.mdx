# Cellular Level of Organization

> **Lesson focus** - Name the main parts of a cell and their functions, distinguish cytoplasm from cytosol, explain selective permeability, and describe the processes that transport substances across the plasma membrane.

---

# The Plasma Membrane

- A flexible outer surface made of a **lipid bilayer**
- Separates the internal from the external environment
- Has **selective permeability** to nutrients and electrically charged ions

## Membrane Proteins

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Enzymes** | Help catabolize reactions |
| **Ion channels** | Allow certain ions into and out of the cell |
| **Identity markers** | Determine blood type and allow the body to recognize self from non-self (MHC) |
| **Carrier proteins** | Allow transit of certain substances across the membrane |

---

# Transport Across the Plasma Membrane

**Diffusion** is the mixing of particles in a solution that occurs because of the particles' kinetic energy. Particles move from areas of higher concentration to areas of lower concentration.

| Process | Energy requirement |
|---------|--------------------|
| **Passive** | Requires no cellular energy; substances move *down* a concentration or electrical gradient |
| **Active** | Uses cellular energy (ATP) to drive substances *against* a concentration or electrical gradient |

> **Electroneutrality** - the cell maintains an electrically neutral charge to maintain homeostasis.

## Factors Affecting Diffusion Rate

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Steepness of concentration gradient** | Greater difference = greater rate |
| **Temperature** | Higher temperature = faster rate |
| **Mass of diffusing substance** | Larger mass = slower rate |
| **Surface area** | Larger area = faster rate |
| **Diffusion distance** | Greater distance = slower rate |

## Passive Processes

### Simple Diffusion

- Does not require the assistance of membrane proteins
- **Osmosis** - the net movement of a solvent (H₂O) through a selectively permeable membrane; occurs from areas of high pressure to low pressure
- The solute within the solution also exerts a force called **osmotic pressure**

### Facilitated Diffusion

- Diffusion that occurs via ion channels or carrier-mediated channels

## Clinical Application: Tonicity

| Solution | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Hypotonic** | Lower concentration of solute than is found inside normal cells (↓ solute; ↑ solvent) |
| **Isotonic** | Solute concentration and water concentration are the same compared to body fluids (solute = solvent) |
| **Hypertonic** | Higher concentration of solute than is found inside normal cells (↑ solute; ↓ solvent) |

> **Quick tips:** solute is typically salt, solvent is typically water, and *water follows salt*.

# Cytoplasm

The **cytoplasm** is all of the cellular contents between the plasma membrane and the nucleus.

- **Cytosol** - the fluid portion of the cytoplasm
- **Organelles** - "little organs"

## Organelles

| Organelle | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Cilia / flagella** | Allow movement and attachment of organisms |
| **Ribosomes** | Sites of protein synthesis |
| **Rough ER** | A network of flattened sacs lined with ribosomes; responsible for protein synthesis |
| **Smooth ER** | Steroid and fatty acid synthesis; detoxifies potentially harmful substances |
| **Golgi body complex** | Packages and modifies proteins and other molecules; attaches or breaks apart specific molecules based on the cell's or surrounding tissue's needs |
| **Lysosomes** | Responsible for breaking down other molecules |
| **Mitochondria** | The "powerhouse of the cell"; generates the majority of the cell's ATP |

> The **endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** is a network of membranes in the form of flattened sacs or tubules. **Cristae** are the ridges within the mitochondria and are the site of cellular respiration / the Krebs cycle.

# Nucleus

The **nucleus** houses most of the cell's DNA.
