Basic Anatomical Terminology
• Lateral vs Medial
- Lateral: farther from midline
- Medial: nearer to midline
• Proximal vs Distal
- Proximal: nearer to the attachment / origination of the structure
- Distal: farther from the attachment / origination of the structure
• Superficial vs Deep
- Superficial: toward or on the surface of the body
- Deep: away from the surface of the body
• Superior vs Inferior
- Superior: toward the head or upper part of the structure
- Inferior: away from the head or lower part of the structure
• Anterior vs Posterior
- Anterior: toward the front
- Posterior: toward the back

Medical Imaging
• Radiography - single barrage of x-rays passes through the body producing an image
of interior structures on x-ray sensitive film
§ Inexpensive
§ Quick results
§ Bones appear white; air/hollow structures appear black
§ Contrast can be added to make some structures more prominent
 
 
Medical Imaging
Chest X - Ray (CXR)
§ Most rapid and utilized to identify lung issues
 
 
Medical Imaging
Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)
§ Body is exposed to high energy magnetic field and radio waves
which creates patterns read by computer
Pros Cons
Relatively safe Cannot be used on patients with
metal in body
Fine detail of soft tissue No fine detail for bones
Great for distinguishing
abnormal/normal tissue
 
 
Medical Imaging
MRI
• used to detect tumors, artery-
clogging fatty plaques, and brain
abnormalities
 
 
Medical Imaging
Computed Tomography (CT)
• Uses xrays to form transverse sectional imaging
• Visualizes soft tissue and organs in detail
• Multiple scans can be assembled to create 3D image
• Beneficial for screening lung cancers, CAD, and kidney cancers
 
 
Medical Imaging
Ultrasound
High frequency sound waves produce image when they reflect off object
• Safe/noninvasive/painless/no dyes
• Most commonly used to visualize fetus
• Also used for size, location, and actions of organs and blood flow through vessels (Doppler)
 
 
Medical Imaging
Coronary (Cardiac) Computed Tomography Angiography Scan (CCTA)
• Iodine containing medium is injected into vein
• Xrays beams create 3D image of heart and vessels
• Primary use: identify coronary artery blockage
 
 
Medical Imaging
Positron Emission Tomography (PET)
• Substance injected into body and absorbed by tissues
• Used to study physiology of body structures such as metabolism
in brain or heart
 
 
Medical Imaging
Endoscopy
• Involves visual exam of inside of body organs or cavities
• Therapeutic and diagnostic purposes
Example: Bronchoscopy

Carrier Proteins - allow transit of certain substances across membrane
 
 
Transport Across the Plasma Membrane
Diffusion – mixing of particles in a solution occurs because of the particle’s kinetic energy
Particles will move from areas of higher concentration to area of lower concentration
1. 2. Passive - requires no cellular energy; substances move down concentration or electrical gradient
Electroneutrality - cell maintains electrically neutral charge to maintain
homeostasis
Active - cellular energy is used to drive substances against concentration or electrical gradient;
requires ATP
 
 
Transport Across the Plasma Membrane
Factors affecting diffusion rate (permeability of particles)
1. Steepness of concentration gradient
greater difference = grater rate
2. Temperature
higher temp = faster rate
3. Mass of diffusing substance
larger mass = slower rate
4. Surface area
larger area= faster rate
5. Diffusion distance
greater distance = slower rate
 
 
Transport Across Plasma Membrane
Passive Processes:
Simple Diffusion - does not require assistance of membrane proteins
- Osmosis: net movement of a solvent (H2O) through selectively
permeable membrane; occurs from areas of high pressure to low
pressure
*Note: the solute within the solution will also exert a force called
osmotic pressure
Facilitated Diffusion - diffusion occurs via
ion channels or carrier-mediated channels
 
 
Transport Across the Cell Membrane

blood (↑solute; ↓solvent)
• Hypertonic solutions – higher concentration of solute than is found inside normal cells and

Contraction and Relaxation of Skeletal Muscle Fibers
Muscle Proteins
1. Contractile proteins: generate force during contraction
- actin
- myosin
2. Regulatory proteins: turn contraction on and off
- tropomyosin: prevents myosin from binding to actin
- troponin: regulated by Ca++; controls attachment of tropomyosin
3. Structural proteins: keep proper structure, elasticity, and extensibility
- titin
- myomesin
- nebulin
- dystrophin
 
 
Contraction and Relaxation of Skeletal Muscle Fibers
Sliding Filament Mechanism
Skeletal muscle shortens during contraction due to thick (myosin) and thin (actin)
filaments sliding past one another
 
 
Contraction and Relaxation of Skeletal Muscle Fibers
The Contraction Cycle
1. ATP hydrolysis
2. Attachment of myosin to actin
3. Power stroke
4. Detachment of myosin from actin
 
 
Contraction and Relaxation of Skeletal Muscle Fibers
ATP Hydrolysis
• Phosphate bond on ATP is broken by water and energy is released
• Products: ADP + Phosphate group
• Energy stored in myosin head
 
 
Contraction and Relaxation of Skeletal Muscle Fibers
Attachment of Myosin to Actin
• Energized myosin head attaches to myosin binding site
• Phosphate group is released
 
 
Contraction and Relaxation of Skeletal Muscle
Fibers
Power Stroke
• Myosin head pivots
• Thin filament pulls past the thick filament
• ADP is released from the myosin head
 
 
Contraction and Relaxation of Skeletal Muscle
Fibers
Detachment of myosin from actin
• Regulatory proteins turn contraction “off”
• ATP binds to myosin head
• Myosin releases from actin

• Synergistic effect- effects of 2+ hormones acting together is greater than effect of each hormone

• Antagonistic effect- effect of one hormone on a target cell is opposed by another hormone
