# The Cardiovascular System

## Components of Blood

### Plasma

- A watery extracellular matrix; the straw-colored liquid that remains after the formed elements are separated
- **91.5% water**, **8.5% solutes**
- Includes hepatocytes, albumins, globulins, fibrinogen, and antibodies
- Often donated for clotting factors

### Formed Elements

- **RBCs (erythrocytes)**
- **WBCs (leukocytes)**
- **Platelets**

## Functions and Properties of Blood

| Function | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Transportation** | O₂/CO₂ exchange, nutrients from the digestive canal, hormones, and waste products to eliminating organs |
| **Regulation** | pH through buffers; adjusts body temperature through water |
| **Protection** | Coagulation properties, antibodies, and WBCs/phagocytes |

## Red Blood Cells (Erythrocytes)

- Contain **hemoglobin**, the oxygen-carrying protein
- Reversible binding (O₂/CO₂)

## White Blood Cells

- Perform phagocytosis and the immune response
- Normally **5,000–10,000 per microliter** of blood
- Increased in times of stress (infection, surgery, illness, etc.)

## Hemostasis

**Hemostasis** is the sequence of events that stops bleeding and prevents hemorrhage:

1. Vascular spasm
2. Platelet plug formation
3. Blood clotting

## Basic Anatomy of the Cardiovascular System

| Vessel | Function |
|--------|----------|
| **Arteries** | Carry blood away from the heart (generally oxygenated) |
| **Veins** | Carry blood back to the heart (generally deoxygenated) |

## Pathway of Circulation

| Circulation | Pathway |
|-------------|---------|
| **Systemic** | Oxygenated blood flows from the left ventricle (via the aorta) to all organs, and deoxygenated blood returns to the right atrium |
| **Pulmonic** | Deoxygenated blood flows from the right ventricle to the lungs (via the pulmonary artery) and returns oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium (via the pulmonary veins) |

# The Lymphoid System

## The Lymphatic System

The lymphatic system consists of lymph fluid and vessels as well as major organs, and it allows immune responses to occur.

- **Spleen** - the largest mass of lymph tissue in the body
- **Red bone marrow**
- **Lymph nodules** (e.g., tonsils)

## Concept of Immunity

**Immunity** is the ability to ward off damage or disease through defenses.

## Function of the Lymphatic System

- Drains excess interstitial fluid from tissue spaces and returns it to the blood
- Transports nutrients absorbed by the digestive canal
- Carries out immune responses

## Innate vs. Adaptive Immunity

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Innate** | Present at birth |
| **Adaptive** | Involves specific recognition of a microbe once it has breached the innate defenses |

# The Respiratory System

## Overview of Respiration

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Pulmonary ventilation** | The act of breathing; air goes in and out |
| **External respiration** | Gas exchange between the alveoli and pulmonary capillaries |
| **Internal respiration** | Gas exchange between blood in the systemic capillaries and tissue cells (perfusion) |

## Upper Respiratory System

| Structure | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Nose** | Provides humidity and olfactory senses |
| **Nasal cavity** | A large space lined with mucous membranes; produces mucus and traps debris |
| **Oral cavity** | — |
| **Pharynx (throat)** | Houses the tonsils |

## Lower Respiratory Tract

- Larynx (voice box)
- Trachea
- Lungs
- Bronchi

# The Digestive System

## Functions

- Breaks food into smaller molecules that can be used by cells
- Muscular contractions:
  - Break down food to move it throughout the body
  - Mix food with digestive enzymes

## Notable Organs

### The Liver

- Composed of **hepatocytes**:
  - Nutrient metabolism
  - Detoxification
  - Phagocytosis of bacteria in the blood (**Kupffer cells**)
- The first organ to absorb/metabolize nutrients from the GI tract:
  - Carbohydrates: glycogen → glucose
  - Lipids → triglycerides / fatty acids
  - Proteins → amino acids (used for ATP production)

### The Pancreas

- Carbohydrates: glycogen → glucose
- **Low** blood glucose stimulates secretion of **glucagon**
- **High** blood glucose stimulates **insulin**

# The Urinary System

## Functions of the Kidneys

1. Excretion of waste (urine)
2. Regulation of blood ionic composition (Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻, HPO₄)
3. Regulation of blood pH
4. Regulation of blood volume
5. Regulation of blood pressure
6. Production of hormones
7. Regulation of blood glucose level

### Regulation of Blood pH

- Excrete excess H⁺
- Conserve bicarbonate for buffer

### Regulation of Blood Pressure

| Mediator | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Anti-diuretic hormone (ADH)** | Increases when BP is low; decreases when BP is high |
| **Renin** | — |

### Production of Hormones

| Hormone | Role |
|---------|------|
| **Calcitriol** | The active form of vitamin D; regulates Ca²⁺ homeostasis |
| **Erythropoietin (EPO)** | Stimulates production of RBCs; becomes reduced during kidney failure |

## The Nephron - Anatomy

The **nephron** is the functional unit of the kidney.

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Renal corpuscle** | Filters blood plasma; contains the glomerulus (capillary network) and the glomerular (Bowman's) capsule (double-walled epithelium) |
| **Renal tubule** | The site of major buffer (H⁺ and HCO₃⁻) action for the kidneys; includes the proximal convoluted tubule, the nephron loop (loop of Henle), and the distal convoluted tubule |

## The Nephron - Physiology

| Process | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Glomerular filtration** | Water and solutes move across the glomerular capillaries; filtration of nutrients occurs |
| **Tubular reabsorption** | Renal tubular cells reabsorb 99% of filtered water and other solutes; reabsorbed nutrients return to the blood |
| **Tubular secretion** | Waste, drugs, and excess ions are secreted out of the body via the renal tubule and duct cells |
