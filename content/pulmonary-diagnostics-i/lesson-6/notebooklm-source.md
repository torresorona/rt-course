## Topic 1: Bronchoscopy Definition

Bronchoscopy is a method for directly visualizing pathologic changes in the trachea and bronchi.

## Topic 2: Diagnostic Bronchoscopy

Diagnostic bronchoscopy may be used when there is suspicion of bronchogenic carcinoma, an abnormal chest X-ray such as a mass, symptoms such as wheezing, cough, chest pain, or dyspnea, positive sputum cytologic findings, or hemoptysis.

Chronic bronchitis and bronchiectasis are the most common causes of hemoptysis. Bronchogenic carcinoma and tuberculosis are the second most common causes. Pulmonary infarction, pulmonary hypertension, pneumonia, sarcoidosis, and lung abscesses can also cause hemoptysis.

## Topic 3: Therapeutic Bronchoscopy

Therapeutic bronchoscopy may be used for removal of foreign bodies, aspiration and removal of thick secretions, administration of localized medications, and selective lavage such as BAL.

## Topic 4: Bronchoscopy Contraindications

Relative contraindications include patient unable to cooperate, tracheal obstruction or uncontrolled asthma, cardiovascular instability, and moderate to severe hypoxemia.

Absolute contraindications include absence of signed consent, absence of experienced personnel, lack of emergency equipment, and inability to adequately oxygenate.

## Topic 5: Bronchoscopy Hazards

Hazards include hemorrhage, pneumothorax, hypoxemia, cardiac arrhythmia, bronchospasm or laryngospasm especially in asthmatic patients, and reaction to topical anesthesia.

Hemorrhage and pneumothorax account for the majority of deaths associated with bronchoscopy.

## Topic 6: Bronchoscopy Equipment

Equipment includes fiberoptic bronchoscope with light source, bronchoscopy cart with necessary supplies, suction, crash cart, oxygen, and pulse oximeter and/or EKG monitor.

## Topic 7: Bronchoscopy Medications

Atropine reduces salivation, bronchial secretions, and vasovagal reflex.

Versed or Demerol may relax the patient. Romazicon reverses Versed, and Narcan reverses Demerol if the patient is overmedicated.

Patients with asthma may receive a bronchodilator to prevent or minimize airway obstruction.

Sodium chloride solution is used for irrigation and injectable solution. Lidocaine solutions may range from 1-10%. Topical anesthetic sprays may include benzocaine spray, Ceta Caine, Hurricaine, Exacta Cain, and Topex.

Methylene blue treats methemoglobinemia from overuse of benzocaine spray. Epinephrine 1:1000 reduces bleeding.

## Topic 8: Pre-Procedure Preparation

Consent should be signed before bronchoscopy. The patient should fast 4-8 hours before the procedure.

Patients with pulmonary tuberculosis should have 2 weeks of effective chemotherapy before the procedure.

Supplies include syringes and needles, cotton-tipped applicators, 4 x 4 gauze, nasal cannula, suction canister, tubing, tonsil suction, and sputum traps.

## Topic 9: Diagnostic Specimens

Diagnostic bronchoscopy may include biopsies and bronchial lavage.

Microbiology lavage has no solution added. Cytology lavage uses Saccomanno solution in a 50-50 mix.

For brush biopsy of pulmonary lesions, protected brush for microbiology is placed in saline solution. Cytology brush is spread on slides and placed in a slide holder with Saccomanno solution.

Forceps biopsy obtains lung tissue such as tumors. Tissue specimen is placed in formalin. Transbronchial lung biopsy for diffuse interstitial lung disease is done with fluoroscopy, carries a higher risk of hemorrhaging by the patient, and the specimen must float to indicate alveolar tissue.

Needle biopsy may use a Wang needle flushed with Hank's solution.

## Topic 10: Foreign Body Removal Supplies

Grasping baskets have two looped wires for retrieval of rounded objects.

Snare has a single looped wire for retrieval of long, thin objects.

V-shaped forceps remove thin, flat objects. Rat tooth forceps remove flat objects such as coins. Alligator jaw forceps hold or remove objects. Rubber-tipped forceps are for sharp or flat objects such as needles or pins. Three-prong forceps are also used for foreign body removal.

## Topic 11: Bronchoscopy Procedure Support

Wear proper attire: gowns, gloves, mask, and goggles.

If the patient is suspected of having HIV or hepatitis virus, extra care should be taken, such as a full-face shield.

If the patient has MTB, a HEPA filter mask is required. The mask is fitted and issued through Bioenvironmental.

## Topic 12: Airway Anesthesia

Gargling lidocaine can provide analgesia of the glottis and upper airways.

Nebulized lidocaine 2% or 4% provides analgesia of the upper and lower airways.

Viscous lidocaine may be applied to the nasal passages with a cotton-tipped applicator.

For dropper instillation, the patient is seated erect. The mouth and pharynx are sprayed with 1-2% solution, and a curved adapter instills solution to the base of tongue, epiglottis, vocal cords, and trachea.

Overuse of benzocaine sprays can cause methemoglobinemia. No more than two 2-second sprays are recommended.

Spray-as-you-go means lidocaine is instilled through the advancing bronchoscope, usually spraying the vocal cords, trachea, and carina.

## Topic 13: Bronchoscopy Procedure and Post-Procedure

The patient may be supine or reclined in a dental chair.

Place the patient on supplemental oxygen, usually 2-3 L.

Bronchoscopy may be performed transnasally, which is most common, or transorally with a bite block.

Monitor patient status throughout the procedure. Observe for topical anesthesia toxicity, hypoxemia, and cardiac arrhythmias.

Post-procedure, the patient should not eat or drink for 2 hours and should start with small sips of water.

Clean and disinfect equipment by placing it in glutaraldehyde for the manufacturer's required time. Patients suspected of having AIDS, tuberculosis, or hepatitis may require higher-level sterilization.

## Topic 14: Sputum Induction

Sputum induction collects fresh, uncontaminated secretions from the tracheobronchial tree.

The goal is achieved when the alert patient is carefully instructed by a trained health care provider, such as an RT, on the proper collection procedure.

The patient coughs. Hypertonic saline by SVN may be needed for patients having difficulty producing a strong productive cough.

When sputum can be produced, the sample is placed in a sterile cup and sent to the medical laboratory for testing.

If the patient cannot produce a sample, RTs are able to perform tracheobronchial suctioning or assist with bronchoscopic lavage.

Tracheobronchial suctioning uses a sterile suction catheter connected to a suction trap that collects the sample.

Bronchoscopic lavage is performed by a physician during bronchoscopy with assistance from an RT. A suction device is connected to a suction trap that collects the sample.

## Topic 15: Sleep Testing Overview

Sleep disorder tests include polysomnography, overnight pulse oximetry, ambulatory sleep tests or home sleep tests, actigraphy, Multiple Sleep Latency Test or daytime nap study, and Multiple Wakefulness Test.

## Topic 16: Polysomnography Definition and Indications

Polysomnography, or PSG, is an overnight study required for definitive diagnosis of sleep apnea.

Throughout the night, the sleep technologist monitors the patient for sleep-disordered breathing, including diminishing or absent breaths or no attempt to breathe during sleep.

Indications include excessive daytime sleepiness and sleep apnea.

Sleep apnea means repeated episodes of no airflow for at least 10 seconds during sleep.

Obstructive sleep apnea means effort is present but no airflow occurs because of an upper airway obstruction.

Central sleep apnea means the CNS fails to signal respiratory effort.

## Topic 17: Polysomnography Equipment and Interpretation

Several physiologic signals are recorded throughout the sleep study to identify the presence and extent of a sleep disorder.

Signals include EEG for brain waves, EOG for eye movement, chin EMG for teeth grinding, ECG for cardiac rhythm, airflow at the nose and mouth, ventilatory effort using inductive plethysmography or piezoelectric belts, and oxygen saturation by pulse oximetry.

If sleep disturbance is present, its severity and cardiopulmonary dysfunction are monitored.

Obstructive sleep apnea shows respiratory effort detected but no airflow, with or without desaturation.

Central sleep apnea shows no respiratory effort detected and no airflow, with or without desaturation.

Obstructive hypopnea shows respiratory effort detected with minimal airflow, with or without desaturation.

AHI is the number of apneas and hypopneas per hour of sleep.

## Topic 18: CPAP/BiPAP Titration

A titration study is an in-lab sleep study used to calibrate CPAP or BiPAP therapy.

Titrations performed in the sleep lab are ideal for determining the appropriate setting.

CPAP is first-line medical therapy for OSA. Once diagnosed with OSA, the patient needs a titration study before treatment begins.

BiPAP is ideal for patients requiring separate pressures for inhalation and exhalation. It assists in ventilation and airway splinting.

Auto-titrating devices are also called auto-CPAP, intelligent CPAP, or smart CPAP. They adjust CPAP levels based on need and monitoring of snoring, hypopneas, and apneas.

Titration should stop all apneic episodes and reduce the number of hypopneas. It should improve sleep while eliminating breathing-related EEG arousals and micro-arousals.

Patient compliance and comfort are key to CPAP/BiPAP success.

## Topic 19: Other Sleep Tests

Overnight pulse oximetry uses a small device with a finger probe to measure oxygen saturation levels overnight. It is used to determine whether treatment such as CPAP or BiPAP is effective. If periodic desaturation is present, the provider may have the patient return for titration to overcome apnea or hypopnea.

Ambulatory sleep tests, also called home sleep tests, are indicated for diagnosis of obstructive sleep apnea. Equipment is a portable device the size of a telephone.

Actigraphy is a non-invasive method of monitoring human rest/activity cycles. It measures movement of a limb and is worn for a week or more. When polysomnography is not available, actigraphy is indicated to estimate total sleep time in patients with obstructive sleep apnea. It is also used to record circadian rhythms and sleep patterns or disturbances in patients with insomnia and hypersomnia.

## Topic 20: MSLT and MWT

The Multiple Sleep Latency Test, or daytime nap study, is indicated for excessive daytime sleepiness and helps diagnose narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia.

It is a full-day test consisting of five 15-minute naps separated by 2-hour breaks. The test measures how long it takes the patient to fall asleep and whether the patient enters REM sleep. If the patient does not fall asleep in 20 minutes, the nap trial ends.

Narcolepsy is associated with entering REM sleep two or more times during the MSLT. Idiopathic hypersomnia is associated with falling asleep quickly but not entering REM sleep.

The Multiple Wakefulness Test measures how alert a patient is during the day. It determines whether someone with a sleep disorder is responding well to treatment. It is critical when a person's job involves public transportation or safety, such as pilots.

MWT consists of four sleep trials with 2-hour breaks between them. In each trial, the patient sits in bed with head supported and tries to stay awake as long as possible. Trials end if the patient does not fall asleep within 40 minutes. The provider reviews the test to assess the patient's level of daytime sleepiness.
